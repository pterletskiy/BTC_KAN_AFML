BTC Daily Direction Prediction Using KANs Within the AFML Framework

A structured overview of the thesis: the questions it sets out to answer, the contributions it offers, the methodology it follows, the results it produces, and the open points for advisor input.

ISEG Mathematical Finance 2024 / 26 Petr Terletskiy · l63023 Supervisor: Prof. João Afonso Bastos
Section 01 Executive Summary

The cryptocurrency machine-learning literature reports daily Bitcoin direction accuracies between 85% and 95%. These figures rarely survive scrutiny: they rest on naive train/test splits, fixed-time-horizon labels, and overlapping observations that leak future information into training. This thesis re-evaluates the question under the full Advances in Financial Machine Learning framework (López de Prado, 2018), which eliminates those pathways through CUSUM event sampling, triple-barrier labels, sample weights, Combinatorial Purged Cross-Validation, and a statistical-correction layer (Deflated Sharpe Ratio, Probability of Backtest Overfitting, DeLong AUC). Six models are benchmarked under identical conditions: AR Logistic, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, LSTM, and Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KAN), evaluated on a 73-feature universe spanning technical, statistical, macroeconomic, crypto-macro, on-chain, and lag families.

The novel contribution sits on top of the benchmark. From the CPCV-trained KAN, the thesis extracts a closed-form symbolic expression for P(Up) by pruning weak edges and replacing surviving spline activations with mathematical primitives (polynomial, tanh, trigonometric, exponential). No prior work in the crypto-ML literature delivers a fully cross-validated symbolic direction-prediction formula in a weak-signal financial regime. The formula is what distinguishes this thesis from a standard model-benchmarking study. It delivers an explicit, human-readable mathematical expression for the decision function, something neither ensemble methods nor standard deep learning architectures produce.

Section 02 Problems & Research Questions

Four questions motivate the thesis. They move from is the problem tractable through what works empirically to can we make the winning model interpretable.

Q1 Does BTC daily direction remain predictable under leakage-free evaluation?

Many studies on BTC direction prediction report 85% to 95% accuracy. These studies typically use evaluation methods that allow information from the future to contaminate training, through look-ahead bias and overlapping labels.

Under a leakage-free evaluation protocol, does daily direction remain predictable, or does the reported accuracy collapse?

Q2 Which feature families carry directional signal? Are on-chain features a free lunch?

Predictive features for BTC fall into four families:

  • Technical: derived from price and volume (RSI, MACD, ATR, volatility estimators).
  • Macro: interest rates, commodities, equity indices, yield curves.
  • On-chain: blockchain data unique to crypto (active addresses, exchange flows, miner balances).
  • Statistical: rolling measures of return distributions (entropy, fractality, structural breaks).

Which families carry predictive signal once look-ahead bias is removed? In particular, do on-chain features add information beyond what off-chain features already capture, or is the blockchain's transparency a free lunch that price and macro data can replicate?

Q3 How does KAN compare against the standard model families on the same protocol?

To answer this, KAN is benchmarked against four standard model families on the same protocol, the same inputs, the same sample weights, and the same metrics:

  • Econometric benchmark: AR Logistic Regression (lagged returns only).
  • Linear ML benchmark: Logistic Regression (full feature set, linear).
  • Tree-based ML benchmark: Random Forest and XGBoost (nonlinear, ensemble).
  • Deep learning benchmark: LSTM (sequential, recurrent).

Does KAN outperform any of these architectures, and is the difference statistically distinguishable from chance?

Q4 Can a closed-form formula be extracted from the trained KAN?

Standard neural networks are black boxes: they predict accurately but cannot explain why. KANs are different by design. Their internal activation functions can be replaced with closed-form mathematical expressions such as polynomials, tanh, or exponentials, producing an explicit formula for P(Up) directly in terms of the input features.

Can such a formula be extracted from the trained KAN, and is it faithful enough to the trained model to serve as a transparent diagnostic of its decision logic?

Section 03 Solution & Contributions

Each contribution maps directly to one research question. Together they constitute the deliverable: a leakage-free evaluation of BTC direction prediction with a novel interpretability layer on the focal model.

C1 Honest evaluation of BTC predictability under AFML

The thesis adopts the full AFML framework (López de Prado, 2018), from labeling through cross-validation through statistical correction of reported metrics. This eliminates the look-ahead bias and overlapping-label problems that inflate accuracy in the standard crypto-ML literature.

The contribution is methodological honesty: a measurement of how predictable BTC daily direction actually is, when the evaluation protocol does not leak future information into training.

C2 A 73-feature universe spanning all four families

The thesis assembles a 73-feature universe: 25 technical indicators, 9 statistical measures (entropy, Hurst exponent, fractality, structural breaks), 20 macroeconomic series, 1 crypto-macro series, 8 on-chain features from CoinMetrics, and 10 lag features. The breadth alone is a contribution; most crypto-ML papers restrict themselves to one or two families.

To separate signal from noise, the protocol tracks how often each feature is selected across the 28 cross-validation folds; persistently selected features carry information, persistently dropped features are noise. This stability ranking is what answers the on-chain question empirically, and feeds the symbolic-extraction stage in Phase III.

C3 Apples-to-apples model benchmark across six architectures

Six models compete on the identical evaluation protocol with the same inputs, the same sample weights, and the same metrics: AR Logistic (econometric), Logistic Regression (linear ML), Random Forest and XGBoost (tree-based ML), LSTM (deep learning), and KAN (focal model).

Each tuned model receives a nested hyperparameter search inside every training fold, so the ranking reflects each architecture's tuning capacity rather than its starting configuration. Pairwise DeLong (1988) AUC tests determine which model differences are statistically distinguishable from chance, where AUC (Area Under the ROC Curve) measures how well a classifier ranks positive cases above negative ones. The contribution is the apples-to-apples comparison that the crypto-ML literature rarely provides.

C4 Closed-form formula extraction from the cross-validated KAN

A KAN's internal activations are univariate functions sitting on each network edge. After training, the procedure matches each function against a candidate library (polynomials, tanh, exponentials, trigonometric) and replaces it with the closed-form expression of best fit.

The thesis implements Algorithm 1 from the VIX KAN forecasting paper: train, prune weak edges, symbolify the survivors, then fine-tune to absorb substitution error. The output is an explicit closed-form expression for P(Up | features).

The contribution is novel: no prior work extracts a closed-form direction-prediction formula from a fully cross-validated KAN in a weak-signal financial regime. The formula is what distinguishes this thesis from a standard model-benchmarking study.

Section 05 Results

Five deliverables will populate this section, mapping back to the research questions in Section 02. The numbers are not in yet; the items below describe what will be reported and which question each deliverable answers.

R1 Model Comparison Table

The headline summary across the six models, ranked by median path Sharpe (std Sharpe as tiebreaker, preferring consistency). Columns include median Sharpe, std Sharpe, DSR, mean F1, mean accuracy, mean log-loss, mean AUC-ROC, median maximum drawdown, median cumulative return, median win rate, median profit factor.

This is the primary answer to Q3: where does KAN sit relative to AR Logistic, Logistic Regression, Random Forest, XGBoost, and LSTM under the same evaluation protocol?

R2 Equity Curves

For each model, the 7 reconstructed CPCV backtest paths plotted as cumulative-return time series across 2014 to 2026. Visual companion to the comparison table.

The shape of the curves reveals path dispersion: do all 7 paths look similar (consistent strategy) or do they diverge (regime-dependent)? This is the visual diagnostic that surfaces regime-fluke risk before the formal statistical-robustness layer flags it.

R3 Bet Sizing Analysis

The S-curve bet-sizing layer between calibrated probability and trading position, reported per model:

  • Abstention rate: fraction of events where |bet| < 0.05, meaning no trade is taken despite a prediction.
  • Mean and median absolute bet size among traded events.
  • Share of bets at the 0.75 cap, indicating how often the highest-confidence layer is reached.
  • Long/short balance, surfacing any directional bias remaining after calibration.

Connects calibration quality to financial performance: a model that abstains too aggressively loses signal, one that bets near the cap on every prediction is overconfident.

R4 Statistical Robustness · DSR, PBO, DeLong AUC

The AFML statistical-correction layer answers Q1: under leakage-free evaluation, does any model survive multiple-testing correction?

  • Deflated Sharpe Ratio per model. Corrects observed Sharpe for selection bias (six trial models) and non-normal returns. A DSR above 0.95 means the result survives correction.
  • Probability of Backtest Overfitting across the 6-model ensemble. The probability that the in-sample best is the out-of-sample loser. Below 0.3 = robust selection; above 0.5 = anti-predictive.
  • DeLong pairwise AUC test. A 6×6 matrix of p-values for the null hypothesis that two models have the same AUC. Reports how many of the 15 pairs are statistically distinguishable at α = 0.05.
R5 Symbolic Formula by KAN

The novel deliverable, addressing Q4. For the chosen extraction fold (the most recent CPCV fold), the section reports:

  • Pruned KAN architecture, e.g., compression from [73, 6, 2] at training time down to [3, 3, 2] after pruning and the top-3 feature filter.
  • The 3 surviving features in the final formula, ranked by selection frequency across all 28 KAN folds.
  • Closed-form expression for P(Up), written as 1 / (1 + exp(-decision_function)) with the decision function expanded as a sum of polynomial, tanh, and trigonometric terms in the surviving features.
  • Pre-symbolic vs post-symbolic accuracy, quantifying the substitution-error gap.
  • Symbolification rate, the fraction of edges successfully replaced with closed-form functions.

This formula is what differentiates the thesis from a standard model-benchmarking study: an explicit, interpretable mathematical expression for a cross-validated direction predictor.

Section 06 Discussion & Next Steps

Five honest limitations of the protocol. Two come from compute and data constraints; three from deliberate trade-offs in the symbolic-extraction layer and the evaluation aggregation. Each frames a future-work direction.

L1 Computational Power

The compute envelope was sufficient for the experiments reported but constrained the depth of the protocol. With more compute, several knobs would be turned up:

  • More random seeds per model: currently 5 uniform across all six models (up from the earlier asymmetric 2 / 3 configuration). Ten or more would tighten seed-variance estimates further and reduce noise in path-level Sharpe medians.
  • More Optuna trials per fold: currently 40. One hundred or more would explore the search space more thoroughly and reduce the chance that the tuning step misses a productive region.
  • Wider hyperparameter ranges: currently capped (KAN width1 ≤ 6, LSTM hidden_size ∈ {16, 32}, tree depth ≤ 6) to match parameter count to fold size. A larger compute budget supports a more honest "let the data decide" approach with looser caps.

The current results are honest under the available budget; with more compute, they would be tighter, not different in kind.

L2 Daily OHLCV Data Only

The thesis uses daily bars, which match the economic horizon of the macro and on-chain features and keep the sample size manageable. But daily resolution discards intraday microstructure: opening gaps, intraday volatility clustering, order-flow signals visible only at minute or hour resolution.

An intraday extension would let the same AFML pipeline run on hourly bars (or finer), with substantially more events for triple-barrier labeling and a different feature set (microstructure-aware indicators, intraday volatility estimators). This is also the most natural future-work direction since the pipeline architecture is timeframe-agnostic; only the calendar conventions and feature definitions need adaptation.

L3 Symbolic Extraction Is Fold-Specific

The closed-form formula reported in the results is extracted from a single CPCV fold (the most recent), not aggregated across the 28 folds. The formula reflects one fold's signal structure rather than a stable invariant of BTC dynamics.

A more rigorous deliverable would be either of two extensions:

  • Per-fold formulas: extract a formula on each of the 28 folds, then compare which features and which functional forms recur. Persistent features and persistent primitives are evidence of structural signal; one-off features are noise.
  • Symbolic-primitive meta-analysis: across all 15 extractions, count how often each primitive (tanh, polynomial degree, trigonometric, exponential) appears, even when feature identities differ. Recurrent primitives reveal the functional shape of the signal.

Both extensions are tractable but were excluded for thesis scope.

L4 Architectural Simplifications During Symbolic Extraction

To produce a readable formula, the symbolic-extraction model uses three simplifications relative to the CPCV-tuned KAN:

  • Single hidden layer: the tuned width2 is dropped, since two hidden layers produce nested compositions that overwhelm SymPy simplification.
  • Coarse spline grid: grid = 3 instead of 5, producing cleaner symbolic matches with fewer candidate knots.
  • Top 3 features only: the most stable features by selection frequency, restricting formula complexity.

This makes the extracted formula a lower bound on what a full-architecture KAN learns: post-symbolic accuracy is necessarily below pre-symbolic accuracy, and below the CPCV KAN accuracy reported in the model comparison. The trade-off between fidelity and interpretability is deliberate and disclosed transparently. The gap matters for any downstream user who would deploy the formula instead of the full model.

L5 No Regime-Conditional Analysis

The pipeline produces one number per metric per model, averaged across the full 2014 to 2026 period. But BTC has gone through several distinct regimes in this window:

  • Pre-2017 retail bull
  • 2018 winter
  • 2020 to 2021 institutional bull
  • 2022 collapse
  • 2023 to 2024 ETF-driven recovery

A model with median Sharpe of zero across the full sample might still have positive Sharpe in one regime and negative in another. The methodology chapter's path-dispersion diagnostic (top-K-return concentration) addresses this partially, but a formal regime-conditional Sharpe and DSR analysis is left for future work. The argument matters even more if the headline DSR turns out to be near zero: regime-conditional results may reveal pockets of predictability that the full-sample number obscures.
